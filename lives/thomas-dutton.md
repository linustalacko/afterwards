Thomas Dutton first appears on Tuesday 24 November 1857, in the New Court at the Old Bailey, before the Common Serjeant and the seventh jury. The case before his was William Wilkinson’s, for unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin, and Thomas Dutton, aged fifty-two, was indicted “for a like offence.” He pleaded guilty and was confined six months.[^1857] That is the whole of the report. A later certificate of the conviction, read out in court, says he pleaded guilty “to passing counterfeit coin,”[^1858a] and his licence of 1861 says the six months were served in a house of correction, without saying which.[^licence]

![His first trial, as the Proceedings printed it: the day’s heading, then William Wilkinson’s case and his. Image: Harvard University Library, through Old Bailey Online, used non-commercially.](record-dutton-1857.jpg)

If he served the whole six months he was out by about the end of May 1858. On Thursday 2 September 1858, about twelve o’clock in the day, he came into the Robin Hood public house with a woman. Its keeper, Joseph Dodson, served her half a quartern of gin, and she paid with a sixpence. He told her it was bad. She said she had not known, and that she had taken it at the City of London public house, which was near. He offered to go back there with her and set it right; she refused, and paid for the gin in coppers. Thomas Dutton was standing there. Dodson said that the two of them drank out of the same glass.[^1858a][^1858b]

Dodson followed them out into Queen Street, Golden Square. They talked; Thomas Dutton went into the Devonshire Arms and the woman waited outside. There he asked the barmaid, Louisa Agnes Miles, for half a pint of porter, and gave her a sixpence. She broke it in two, told him it was bad, and asked whether he had any more like it. He did not say anything, but gave her a good one, and she gave him fivepence change. She kept one half of the broken coin; he took the other half in his hand and went away. Asked in court whether he had seemed very much surprised, she said: “No; he did not say he was surprised, he never said anything.”[^1858a][^1858b]

Dodson went into the Devonshire Arms, saw the broken coin, and followed the two again. Near Regent Circus in Piccadilly he pointed them out to a policeman, Thomas Sutherland, G 179. Sutherland followed them for about an hour: towards Dover Street, through Bear Street, into a public house in Princes Street, and on to a house near Leicester Square, which the barmaid called the Camden Stores, Castle Street, in one trial and the Camden Arms in the other.[^1858a][^1858b]

There, between one and two o’clock, Thomas Dutton asked the barmaid, Ann Wade, for a pint of stout (in the second trial, a pint of beer) and gave her a sixpence. She put it in the till, where there was one other sixpence. The woman came in and stood beside him. Then Sutherland came in and asked the barmaid what this man had given her. She said a sixpence. Thomas Dutton said it was a shilling. She looked in the till and found the two sixpences, one of them bad, and could not say which was his. Sutherland said he would search him immediately, and Thomas Dutton took out another bad sixpence himself. No bad money was found on him at the station. On the woman were found a shilling, three fourpenny pieces and tenpence in copper.[^1858a][^1858b]

The woman was Caroline Murphy. When the barmaid said the coin was bad, she said, “You shall not be at the loss of it, I will pay you,” and paid her in coppers. Sutherland heard her say, “They can’t hang us for it.”[^1858b]

Whether she was his wife no record says. Both barmaid and publican were asked. Ann Wade said their conduct was like that of man and wife, “I judged that from the way in which they addressed each other—they drank out of the same pot.” Dodson said he had no reason to suppose they were not husband and wife, and, asked whether he always thought so of a man and woman who came in together, “Not always; not if they drink out of the same glass.”[^1858b] Three years later, the prison’s record of him says he was married, and names as his next of kin a Mary Vernon, of 7 Bridgewater Square, Barbican.[^licence] Who she was to him it does not say.

![His second trial begins: the certificate of the 1857 conviction, and the evidence of Joseph Dodson and Louisa Agnes Miles. Image: Harvard University Library, through Old Bailey Online, used non-commercially.](record-dutton-1858-trial.jpg)

He was committed for trial at the police court in Marlborough Street on Thursday 23 September, twenty-one days after the arrest.[^licence] He was tried twice in the New Court on Tuesday 26 October 1858, the second day of the session, before the Common Serjeant and the sixth jury, with Mr Bodkin and Mr Clerk prosecuting. The first trial was his alone, for uttering counterfeit coin having been convicted before; Sutherland produced the certificate of 1857 and said, “the prisoner is the person—I took him in custody.” The second was his and Caroline Murphy’s, for uttering. A Mr Sleigh cross-examined in both; it was in cross-examination that the barmaid was asked whether he had seemed surprised, and the witnesses whether the two were man and wife. The inspector of coin to the Royal Mint, William Webster, said that the coins were all counterfeit, and that the half sixpence from the Devonshire Arms came from the same mould as the one passed at the Camden Arms.[^1858a][^1858b]

Both were found guilty. Thomas Dutton was sentenced to four years’ penal servitude. The jury recommended Caroline Murphy to mercy, and she was confined six months.[^1858b] The Proceedings give his age as fifty-two, the same as eleven months before.[^1858a]

![Sutherland’s evidence, with Caroline Murphy’s words, and the two sentences. Image: Harvard University Library, through Old Bailey Online, used non-commercially.](record-dutton-1858-verdict.jpg)

The only description of him is in his licence, which I have read as the Digital Panopticon transcribes it and not as a page. He was five feet six inches tall and “stoutish,” with a fair complexion, brown hair and light hazel eyes, an anchor on his right arm and a scar on his right cheek. He was a labourer, a Protestant, married, with two children, and could not read or write. His health in prison was “pretty good.”[^licence] The Digital Panopticon files the anchor among naval tattoos;[^tattoo] whether he had ever been at sea no record I have read says.

The same file lists his prisons. At Newgate he was held two months and ten days, in separate confinement, and his conduct was good. At Millbank he was held nineteen days; his conduct and his character were both good, his trade in the prison was “Picker,” and against his progress at school the clerk wrote: “Not here long enough to decide.”[^licence] Where he was after Millbank the transcription does not say. Four entries in the quarterly returns of the convict prisons for 1858 carry his name, age and offence; I have read only their index.[^returns]

On Tuesday 31 December 1861 he was granted a licence, ticket number 10360, for early release from Woking prison.[^licence][^kew] That was 1,162 days after his trial, and 299 days before four years would have run. In the licence, written three years after he was first called fifty-two, he is fifty-two still.[^licence]

After that I can find nothing of him that is certainly his. He is not in the Old Bailey’s Proceedings again.[^search] The Digital Panopticon offers a death in 1887, matched by name and year of birth alone and marked as possibly not the same man; I could not read it.[^death]

## Where the records disagree

- **His age.** Fifty-two in November 1857, October 1858 and December 1861. The licence index takes a birth year of 1809 from the last; the other records give 1806.
- **The last public house.** Ann Wade called it the Camden Stores, Castle Street, Leicester Square, in the first trial, and the Camden Arms in the second. The Mint’s inspector called it the Camden Arms.
- **What he ordered there.** A pint of stout in the first trial, a pint of beer in the second.
- **The day of conviction.** The Proceedings put his trials on 26 October 1858; the licence and the catalogue give 25 October, the day the session opened.
