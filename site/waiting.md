title: Waiting to be written
---

- **Mary Ann Munday**, twenty-one in 1842, who pawned a stolen pair of boots under another name and got six months.
- **John Tatnell, William Hampshire and Laurence Brennan junior**, three boys whose boots were taken off their feet in one week of October 1842.
- **George Baylis senior**, once a police inspector of Vintry Ward in the City of London, who petitioned in 1842 for his twelve-year-old son not to be transported, and was refused.
- **Jack the sweep**, named by a crying boy in 1819 and acquitted. A database joins him to George Munt, a sweep transported in 1825. Whether they are one boy is not settled.
- **Elizabeth Bosley**, thirteen in 1824, sentenced to death beside Caroline Molineaux and freed from the Penitentiary at Millbank on 31 May 1830.
- **Mrs. Foot**, who, Lydia Western told the court in 1820, gave her St Bartholomew’s Hospital’s sheets as her own perquisites.
- **Charlotte Walker of Ilminster gaol**, seventy-four, sentenced at Bridgwater to fourteen years’ transportation and pardoned in 1840 for her age after five years in gaol (HO 18/26/5). Not the Charlotte Walker of the library.
- **Jane Smith**, twenty-four, who stabbed a Julia Mahoney outside the Royal Sovereign in Bluegate Fields in May 1853, saying she owed her a spite, and was transported for ten years (`t18530509-597`).
- **John Osburn**, about sixty in 1733, who told the parish of St Martin in the Fields that he was born at Guinea in Africa and came to England about the end of Queen Anne’s wars, and was lodging with a trumpeter of the Horse Guards at Marylebone (`smdsset_19_2015`).
- **George Romea**, about twenty in 1777, born at Bengal, who had been footman six years to a surgeon in Coventry Street for clothes, diet and lodging and no wages, and was then in St Martin’s workhouse (`smdsset_32_50698`).
- **Caroline Murphy**, taken with Thomas Dutton in a public house near Leicester Square on 2 September 1858, who told the barmaid “You shall not be at the loss of it, I will pay you.” The policeman heard her say “They can’t hang us for it.” The jury recommended her to mercy, and she was confined six months (`t18581025-957`).
