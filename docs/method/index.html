How a life is written

Something over a hundred billion people have lived. Most left no mark in writing. Of those who did, almost none have had their life told. This library is for them, and it will never be finished.

A life here begins with one record: a trial, a ship’s list, a parish book, a census page. Somebody is named in it, usually for a single day, and nobody since has asked what happened to them afterwards. The library asks, and follows the name through every other record that can be found.

The rules

  • One person at a time. The library is built from single lives, each given whatever attention it takes.
  • Every sentence rests on a record. Each claim carries a note saying where it comes from, and the note links to something you can open. Where the writer is guessing, the sentence says so.
  • Doubt is shown. Two records with the same name are often two people. Each life says why a record was believed, and lists the ones that were turned away.
  • Nothing is improved. A life is not a tribute if it is tidied. What a person did and what was done to them are both kept, in plain words.
  • No life is finished. Each ends with what is not yet known, written as questions someone else can answer.
  • Only the dead. Nobody is written here who died less than a hundred years ago, or who was born less than a hundred and thirty years ago where the death is unknown.
  • Each life says who wrote it and who checked it. Most first drafts are written by an AI reading transcriptions. A life stays marked as unchecked until a person has compared it with the original documents.

Add to the library

There are four ways, from an afternoon to as long as you like. Five, if it is your own family: every life has a place for what a family remembers. All of them go through the library’s page on GitHub.

  • Answer a question. Every life ends with a list of things not yet known. If you can open an archive the library cannot, such as a subscription site, a record office or a newspaper collection, look one up and say what you found.
  • Check a life. Take a life marked unchecked, open the original documents its notes point to, and confirm or correct each one. Report the check and your name goes on the page.
  • Name someone. If you have met a person in a record whose life nobody has followed, an ancestor or a stranger, put them on the waiting list with the record you found them in.
  • Write a life. Follow the rules above and the guide for writers. You can write it yourself or with an AI of your own; either way the page says so, and a second person checks it.

What the library cannot do yet

It reads only archives that are open to everyone and that allow a program to read them. That leaves out most censuses, most newspapers and most parish registers, which sit behind subscriptions or refuse automated readers, and the library does not go round a refusal. It also leaves out everyone who was never written down at all, which is most of the people who have ever lived. For them the library can only say that they were here.
